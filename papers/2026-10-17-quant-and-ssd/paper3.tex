\section*{Abstract}

The second night left a local worker that could finish small fixes once the serving stack was repaired and the
prompt named the file; it left open what else separates a local model from a cloud worker. This third night
narrows the question with nine candidates that ran fresh arms (two more were rejected before grading), paired
precise and plan prompts on the same replay tasks, and one self-directed queue that ran to the morning. Four
findings stand out. First, quantisation decides, and the lift replicates across model families: Ornith-35B-A3B at
8~bit instead of 4~bit turned per-task instability into two concentrated losses (5 of 8 graded arms against 7 of
17, Wilson 31--86\,\%) and produced the first local double pass on box-press; on a second family the same lift
went from 4 of 6 to 5 of 6 on the identical three-task protocol, at half the resident size (17--18\,GB against
35.5). Second, the remaining failure classes are model-side, and one is harness-repairable: dotted tool names
(\cmd{Glob.glob}, \cmd{Read.read}, \cmd{Bash.Bash}) caused all 27 of the night's dotted-name errors, every one in
arms spawned before the repair hook was registered at 19:39 --- a 38-minute registration gap that the failing
arms straddled; invented absolute paths caused 130 path-guard events (97 repaired, 33 denied; 17 distinct
invented roots). Third, speed without instruction-following is worthless: a dense 30B model with the fastest
streams of the night (25--29 tokens/s single stream) passed 0 of 5 graded arms. Fourth, a 125B MoE paged from
SSD is usable (3 of 6, swap flat) but every decode token pays random expert-row page-ins --- 3.0 tokens/s
against 9.8 resident --- and an 80B 8-bit model on the same host never went resident (10.7\,GB rss) and never hit
the prefix cache (0 hits in 102 fetches). The save-model-ref class remains uncrossed (0 of 14 graded local
tries), and its replication attempt wedged the serving process twice (Metal 46.2\,GB active against a 34.6\,GB
limit, resident collapsed to 0.1\,GB). A morning head-to-head then settled the disputed rank-1 seat on the
log-size tiebreak: the 30B-A3B 8-bit passes 4 of 4 gradeable log-size arms against the 35B's 3 of 4, at
3.8--4.4$\times$ the wall speed and roughly 31\,GB resident, and takes rank 1 --- and a daylight build pass over
the same arms (the diffs re-graded, nothing re-run) makes the seat build-verified: all six deciding cells
(log-size 4 of 4, box-press precise 2 of 2, both repetitions) build clean and pass the hidden tests. The
morning's one non-replication cuts the other way: the SSD-paged 125B's box-press precise pass was cap-edge
variance, not capability (rep 2 capped out at 37 turns, 0 files; 1 of 4 over graded box-press arms). An
afternoon replication round then completed the grid --- every task now has two reps: the third quant-lift
family (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, night 1's worst coder at 4~bit) closes its 8-bit record at 10 of 12 over two complete
repetitions, box-press plan the one structural miss, and ornith6's log-size rep flips its night-2 precise fail
to 2 of 2. The
recommendation: the 30B-A3B 8-bit as resident coder, the 35B 8-bit as build-verified fallback, multi-file saves
on the cloud coder.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Night 1 asked whether open-weight models on one workstation can act as agentic coding workers and answered no for
real fixes \citep{Anthropic-2026aa}. Night 2 asked the same question with paired arms and found that the answer
was being suppressed twice: the serving stack never hit its prefix cache, and the worker mistyped paths until a
repair hook was added; with both fixed, a local model finished one-function fixes within about twice the time of
the cloud reference. The question of this third night is narrower still: \emph{with serving and path repair
solved, what else separates a local coder from a cloud coder?} Four candidate answers were put to the test, one
per finding:

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Quantisation.} The MoE candidate of night~2 ran at 4~bit and was unstable per task. The same
	model at 8~bit (Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B, ``ornith8'') is tonight's first hypothesis: the bit width, not the model,
	decides whether the per-task results hold together. When the queue extended in the morning, the same question
	was asked a second time on a different family (Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, 4~bit on night~1 against 8~bit tonight).
	\item \textbf{Model-side failure classes.} The failures that remain after the serving and path fixes --- dotted
	tool names, invented absolute paths, loops --- should now be attributable to the models, and the dotted-name
	class should be repairable in the harness, measured live.
	\item \textbf{Instruction-following, not speed.} A new dense 30B candidate (Muse-Glimmer, 4~bit) with the
	fastest streams of the night tests whether speed alone moves any task.
	\item \textbf{The uncrossed class.} The multi-file, reference-preserving save-model-ref task should remain
	beyond every local candidate, as it was on night~2, and stay with the cloud coder.
\end{itemize}

After the first two candidates and the serving fault of Section~\ref{sec:wedge}, the night turned into a
self-directed queue that ran to the 08:15 close and served four more candidates, two of them experiments in
where a model is allowed to live: a 125B MoE deliberately larger than free memory, paged from SSD, and an 80B
8-bit model that the operating system keeps out of residence entirely --- closed with a log-size
head-to-head that settled the rank-1 seat (Section~\ref{sec:h2h}), and the morning after it, a daylight build
pass verified the seat on the same arms (Section~\ref{sec:daylight}). Section~\ref{sec:changes} describes what changed since
night~2, Section~\ref{sec:setting} the setting, Section~\ref{sec:method} the method, Section~\ref{sec:results}
the results, Section~\ref{sec:harness} the harness findings, and Section~\ref{sec:related} the relation to
published work.

\section{What Changed Since Night 2}\label{sec:changes}

\begin{description}
	\item[The 8-bit arm.] The quantisation A/B of Section~\ref{sec:quant}: the same model family and launcher as
	night~2, at 8~bit instead of 4~bit. The 4-bit model failed its smoke test on night~2 (an assertion error on the
	one-line bug); the 8-bit model passed it.
	\item[Tool-name repair, measured live.] Local models hallucinate namespaced tool names --- \cmd{Glob.glob},
	\cmd{Read.read}, \cmd{Bash.Bash} --- and every such call dies with ``no such tool available'' as a tool-result
	error that no hook saw, so the run thrashes on the same mistake (the same unavailable name sixteen times in one
	arm tonight). A worker-only PreToolUse hook now rewrites a dotted name whose last segment names a known tool,
	case-insensitively, to the bare name and keeps the input; each repair is logged. The rule and the hook were
	committed at 19:01 and the hook was registered live at 19:39; the gap and its lesson are Section~\ref{sec:gap}.
	\item[Finisher discipline.] Night~3 lost arms to phase budgets, not to models: the first ornith8 phase was cut
	by the phase worker's deadline at 18:29 (four arms void, marked not-started), and a second worker deadline at
	19:13 orphaned two glimmer arms mid-run. The rule adopted afterwards: when a phase is cut, the missing
	task--prompt cells are re-run as one completion phase per candidate, paired arms started together, one server
	loaded at a time (glimmer's server stopped 20:02, ornith8 loaded 20:03, stopped 21:33), and the void rows stay
	in the table as history instead of being quietly dropped.
	\item[An ATEM tool parser for Glimmer.] The Muse-Glimmer template carries its own tool-call template
	(\cmd{atem:function\_calls}); no shipped parser of the serving stack, including \cmd{auto}, reads it. A local
	parser was written into the patched launcher and probe-verified against all 38 choices on a sample before the
	server was started. The template raises on string tool arguments, but the serving layer's template-safety
	normalisation already converts them, and no HTTP~500 occurred all night.
	\item[The queue.] After the wedge, the operator extended the night: a worker ran the candidate queue
	self-directed until morning --- the box-press debt run owed by qwen38, gpt-oss-20b, the SSD-paged 125B
	(Section~\ref{sec:ssd}), a 6-bit Ornith completion, the two 8-bit builds of Section~\ref{sec:ranking}, and
	--- at the 08:15 close --- the log-size head-to-head that settled rank~1 (Section~\ref{sec:h2h}).
	Every candidate was smoke-tested (one-line bug through the full tool loop) before any task arm; four of the
	six new servers passed the smoke in 19--68\,s.
\end{description}

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

All measurements ran on the same Apple MacBook Pro with an M5 Max and 128\,GB of unified memory as nights~1 and~2,
set to maximum performance, with the load average logged per arm. Local models were served with vllm-mlx
\citep{vllm-mlx-2026aa} through patched launchers (thinking off where the template supports it, output capped at
8192), one model server at a time. Workers were started through PAI \citep{Nott-2026aa}. The workloads are
anonymised as in the earlier reports: Project~A is the native PDF application that supplies the coding tasks.

One constraint is carried over from the reboot of night~2 and tightened. A \emph{36\,GB watch line} bounds the
resident size of the one allowed server, measured as resident memory of the serving process, not requested:
ornith8 was measured at 35.0\,GB at smoke start and 35.29\,GB at the cut-off of its first phase, and peaked at
35.5\,GB during the completion phase --- half a gigabyte under the line, with the Metal allocation limit set to
34.6\,GB (30\,\% of 115.4\,GB). Glimmer, dense at 4~bit, sat far below it (15.5--16.0\,GB resident, Metal peak

30B class is the only one that leaves the machine headroom. What the watch line does not prevent is
Section~\ref{sec:wedge}: Metal activity can exceed the allocation limit from outside the cache, and that is where
the night's serving fault lives.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Tasks, prompts, grading}

The four replay tasks of nights~1 and~2 are reused unchanged: fixes that already landed in Project~A, started from
the parent commit in an isolated clone, graded by the hidden tests that came with the fix. Deadlines are 20
minutes for view-decimals, 15 for log-size, 25 for box-press and 40 for save-model-ref (30 minutes on the
SSD-paged arms, where every arm was expected to run to its cap); wall time is censored at the deadline and the
build and test exit codes are recorded. Each task runs as a pair of arms started together, the \emph{precise}
prompt (file and function named) against the \emph{plan} prompt (a cloud-written edit plan, ``cloud head, local
hands''). A patch passes when the result builds and the hidden tests pass; no model judges another. Wilson
intervals \citep{Wilson-1927aa} are given for pass rates.

\subsection{Two grading modes, and when each counts}\label{sec:grading}

The night used both graders, and the ranking states which row rests on which. Through the ornith6 completion
(23:37) every verdict is a \emph{build verdict}: the hidden tests ran, and pass means the tests went green ---
these are the same verdicts as nights~1 and~2. In the later queue rounds the builder was dropped (the operator
banned the heavy build as a watch-line risk), and arms were graded by diff comparison against the landed fix:
\emph{check} when the arm touched the fix's own file and function, \emph{likely-fail} otherwise. Checks were then
inspected by hand; every pass quoted for local30x8, ddalcu and codernext is an inspected diff that contains the
true fix (for log-size, the exact one-line root cause), and Section~\ref{sec:limits} carries the cost of this
substitution. Build verdicts and inspected checks are never mixed inside one fraction without a note. After the
close, a daylight build pass re-graded the deciding arms through the builder --- the same diffs, no re-run
(Section~\ref{sec:daylight}).

\subsection{Replication rule}

Night~2's rule ``no single-run verdicts'' is enforced as a replication pass: every cell of the shortlisted
candidate is run a second time (rep~2) before the ranking is called final. Through the ornith8 wedge the pass
was incomplete by a serving fault, not by choice: view-decimals replicated (the split repeated exactly) and the
save-model-ref pair wedged the server twice (Section~\ref{sec:wedge}) and stays void. The queue's rounds then
closed the grid cell by cell --- the head-to-head's log-size rep 2, local30x8's box-press and view-decimals
reps, glm47's log-size rep, ddalcu's box-press rep 2 (which did not replicate,
Sections~\ref{sec:h2h}, \ref{sec:daylight}, \ref{sec:ssd}), and finally every task of the third quant-lift
family a second time plus ornith6's log-size pair (Table~\ref{tab:logrep}). At the 13:33 close every
candidate$\times$task cell is run twice or structurally settled: every task now has two reps, qwen36x8's
8-bit record closes at 10 of 12 (its box-press plan the one structural miss), and ornith6's log-size rep
re-grades its night-2 cells at 2 of 2 buildless. The ranking stands on rep-1 evidence for the candidates the
seat was decided on, with the replicated cells marked, and the sample sizes are stated wherever a fraction is
quoted.

\subsection{Voids}

An arm that never reached the model or was killed by the harness before grading is \emph{void}, never a fail.
Tonight's voids have three causes: phase-budget kills (six arms), a loop-guard kill on a degenerate arm
(Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}), and the wedged server (two arms).

\subsection{The rank-1 head-to-head}\label{sec:h2h}

Round 7 resolved the queue's two remaining candidates before either served --- a GLM-5.2-colibri pack rejected
on model format (a proprietary E8-lattice container no local runtime dequantises, 289\,G on disk against
${\sim}$90\,G free, and its own README's reference host decodes at 1.57\,tok/s) and Qwen3-Coder-480B small MLX
quants skipped on the free-RAM rule (no published quant under ${\sim}$250\,G on disk; the SSD-paging tax was

\subsection{The SSD-overrun experiment}\label{sec:ssd}

The queue's most interesting question was not ``which model'' but ``where may a model live''. Two candidates
deliberately violate the resident-size assumption. The first, ``ddalcu'', is a 125B qwen4\_exp MoE (125B trunk,
51B n-gram table, 4B MTP head) packed at 3.3 effective bits-per-weight --- below the ${\sim}$3.5-bit threshold at
which on-device evaluations start to bend \citep{Song-2025aa} --- 80\,G on disk against free RAM; its n-gram table
is mmapped from an external store and never resident, so the server \emph{must} page weights from SSD during
decode. Three load blockers were found and fixed in sequence before it served at all: an MTP head stored under an
unexpected key (stripped; served without MTP speculation); a mixed 2/3/4/8-bit pack whose config declares only a
global 4~bit (a generated quantisation map of 774 per-tensor entries, proven by a strict lazy load of all 3167
tensors); and folded RMSNorm weights that turn $(1+w)\cdot x$ into $(2+w)\cdot x$ under the serving stack's
formula (monkeypatched to the plain form; the arithmetic sanity check ``$17\times23$'' then answered ``391'').
After that: smoke pass through the full tool loop in 68\,s, zero template errors, 3 of 6 graded arms with
log-size on both prompts and the box-press precise fix's core --- the second-best quality-per-task-attempted of
the night.

\textbf{Rep 2 corrected the surprise.} The box-press precise pass did not replicate. A patient re-run at the
same 30-minute caps (the morning's round 9; an earlier 20-minute attempt had been killed by its own goal cap
and is history, not a verdict) put the precise arm under the cap for the full 30 minutes --- 37 turns, 0 files
touched, 0 commits, every diff metric 0.00 --- where rep 1 had landed the fix's core in 32 turns; the plan arm
failed on both reps (33 turns, 0 files this time). Box-press reads 1 of 4 over graded arms. What does
replicate is the arithmetic of Section~\ref{sec:grading}'s caps: at 2.4\,tok/s average decode this run (max
10.4; rep 1 averaged 3.0), a 30-minute window is only about 60 turns, so the edit has to land near the first
try --- \emph{cap-out risk, not capability}, decides ddalcu's box-press arms, exactly as it decided
view-decimals (0 of 4). The rep-1 precise pass was variance at the edge of the cap, not a stable capability.
The run itself was clean: one server, 57 requests, rss peak 37.1\,GB (rep 1: 32.7), swap flat at 16.9\,GB,
watchdog zero kills. No rank change: ddalcu stays third on log-size 2 of 2 and the inspected rep-1 checks.

The cost is the finding. Over 202 streamed turns, decode averaged 3.0\,tok/s (median 3.3, min 0.7, max 14.2);
the machine survived with swap growing 25\,MB in total against a kill line of 500\,MB per five minutes, and the
server peaked at 32.7\,GB rss (${\sim}$51--57\,G of 128\,G including Metal). But the paging tax is paid on
\emph{every} token, not only cold turns: the first big prefill ran as interleaved SSD reads (1{,}307 slow-step
warnings), and warm turns with a fully cached prefix still decoded at 3--4\,tok/s because MoE decode pages
random expert rows. Every arm ran its full 30-minute cap; at 3\,tok/s that is about 60 turns --- enough for
log-size and box-press, never enough for view-decimals to converge. Speed, not capability, is the SSD-overrun
bottleneck: the same arms that ornith8 finishes at 9.8\,tok/s take a $3\times$ wall-clock tax here.

The second experiment, ``codernext'', is the 80B hybrid-attention MoE at 8~bit (512 experts, 10 active) on the
same host --- and the operating system solved residency its own way: the server process sat at 10.0--10.8\,GB rss
the whole run, the 80\,G of weights were never resident, and macOS paged expert rows from the file cache on
demand. The load was harmless (26--28\,\% free, swap fell 20.0 to 19.1\,G, the guard never fired; loading
displaced ${\sim}$19\,G of other sessions' memory into swap first), the smoke passed in 59\,s --- and the model
managed only 2 of 6, all four view-decimals and box-press arms read-only to the cap at 2.5\,tok/s (about 2
turns per minute), while its two log-size arms again contain the exact one-line fix. It also produced the
night's cleanest cache result: \textbf{zero prefix-cache hits in 102 fetches} over 205 requests (against 166
hits in 2 misses for the qwen3\_moe family the same night on the same launcher), so every turn re-prefills the
whole prompt. Hybrid linear-attention architectures appear to have no working prefix-cache path in this serving
stack; until they have, an 80G resident-free coder pays full prefill on every turn, and the ``pages little at
mixed quantisation'' hope is falsified at pure 8~bit.

\subsection{The serving wedge}\label{sec:wedge}

At about 22:11, one third into the save-model-ref rep2 pair, the ornith8 server stopped inferring: Metal reported
46.2\,GB active with 50.8\,GB peak against the 34.6\,GB allocation limit and an empty (0.0\,GB) cache, the serving
process's resident memory collapsed to 0.1\,GB, and the disconnect guard spun --- one client connection was held
open past 700\,s, a second past 115\,s --- with two requests running and none completing. Both arms were killed
by the arm guard and marked void. The wedge had occurred once before on the same server, identically, which makes
it systematic in the night's sense: long-context arms (save-model-ref has the largest prompt of the four tasks)
trip a vllm-mlx memory path that deadlocks after roughly 35--45 minutes under continuous batching. It is the
third consecutive night whose most expensive finding is a serving fault invisible in any pass/fail table:
night~1's serial prefill, night~2's silent cache defeats, tonight's deadlock. The scheduler's own Metal log line
(active 46.2\,GB) is the diagnostic to watch on future long-context phases; a per-phase Metal-peak watch line is
the cheap lever, and the replication rule absorbed the damage --- the ranking stands on rep~1, and the wedge is
the finding. The queue adopted the consequence directly: no later candidate was run on save-model-ref (cloud
class, wedge risk).

\section{Harness Findings}\label{sec:harness}

\subsection{Dotted tool names, repaired and measured}\label{sec:repair}

The class: a local model writes \cmd{Glob.glob} or \cmd{Read.read} --- namespaced as if tools were modules ---
the client reports ``no such tool available'', and the model retries the same call, in the worst case sixteen
times, until a loop guard kills the arm. The repair: a worker-only PreToolUse hook that, when the tool name
contains a dot and its last segment names a known tool case-insensitively, rewrites the call to the bare name and
keeps the input, logging each rewrite; anything else passes untouched. Measured live tonight: the repair log
holds three rewrites (the registration probe \cmd{Glob.glob} $\rightarrow$ \cmd{Glob} at 19:38:54, and two
rewrites in the very worker that shipped the hook), all 27 dotted-name error rows of the night sit in the two
glimmer arms spawned before 19:39, and the ten arms spawned after it --- including every ornith8 completion
and rep2 arm --- produced none. The class is closed at the cost of one hook and one log line per rewrite.

\subsection{The settings-registration gap}\label{sec:gap}

The hook existed for 38 minutes before it fired: committed at 19:01, registered in the live settings at 19:39,
and hooks are read when a worker spawns. The two box-press arms launched at 19:37 to close glimmer's grid
therefore ran without it and hit the loop the hook exists for; both count as fails or voids in the tables above.
The lesson generalises beyond this hook: \emph{a harness fix is not deployed until the next spawn after its
registration}, so mid-phase fixes must either wait for the phase boundary or be paired with a re-spawn, and any
``the hook did not help'' claim needs a timestamp check against the repair log before it is believed. The probe
rewrites (\cmd{worker=probe} in the log) are the cheap proof of liveness; they should be a standard step of hook
rollout, not an afterthought.

\subsection{Finisher discipline}\label{sec:finisher}

Seven of the night's voids are self-inflicted: six arms were orphaned by phase-worker deadline kills (18:28--18:29
on ornith8, 19:13 on glimmer) and one by the loop guard. None of the seven measures the model. The discipline
adopted after the second kill --- completion phases per candidate, paired arms started together, one server
loaded at a time across the boundary (glimmer stopped 20:02, ornith8 loaded 20:03), voids kept visible in the
table --- recovered every orphaned cell within two hours, which is why the ranking covers all four tasks at all.
The budget--arms interplay is the finding: a phase budget sized for the full grid will always spend its last
minutes killing exactly the arms that a completion phase then has to redo. One interference of the opposite kind
occurred in the morning rounds: both box-press arms of local30x8 were killed from outside the round 50\,s in by
another session's pane activity, with 13 minutes of cap left and no watchdog or deadline involvement; the pair
was re-run clean and completed, the killed attempt's rows stay in the log as history, and the authoritative rows
are the re-run.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

The series' tasks grade agents on real repository fixes with hidden tests, which follows the SWE-bench format
\citep{Jimenez-2024aa}; the harness levers measured tonight (tool-name repair, path denial, loop guard) are
agent-computer-interface interventions in the sense of \citet{Yang-2024aa}, and the precise and plan prompts are
external localisation, the constraint that \citet{Xia-2024aa} identify as binding. The PAI worker layer is the
open-harness counterpart of \citet{Wang-2024ab}. Everything serving-side rests on paged attention and prefix
reuse \citep{Kwon-2023aa}; the radix-tree reuse that SGLang formalises \citep{Zheng-2024aa} is exactly the
mechanism that the night-2 cache defeats broke silently and that codernext shows missing \emph{by architecture}:
a hybrid linear-attention model on which the prefix cache never fires at all.

On quantisation, tonight's A/B is the agentic counterpart of the accuracy--performance trade-off studies: 8~bit
behaves like the near-lossless regime of \citet{Kurtic-2024aa} --- failures concentrate instead of spreading ---
while the low-bit degradation literature \citep{Li-2025aa} locates failures early in reasoning chains, which
matches the 4-bit arm's per-task instability; that quantised models are not uniformly weaker \citep{Fang-2025aa}
is why the arms are paired, and the replication of the lift on a second family is the paired answer. On-device
evaluations that co-report capability with system figures \citep{Song-2025aa} are the single-turn analogue of
Section~\ref{sec:ssd}; the tool-calling angle separates this setting from function-calling benchmarks
\citep{Patil-2024aa,Chen-2024aa} --- a dotted or namespaced tool name costs minutes of thrash inside a
build-test-fix loop, not one point, and the trajectory-level failures (read-only paralysis, protocol confusion)
are invisible to single-turn scoring. Reporting minutes and turns next to pass rates follows the cost-accounting
discipline of \citet{Kapoor-2024aa}; on this night the cost axis is gigabytes and seconds, and it changes the
ranking's conclusion (the cheaper candidate takes rank~1 on the head-to-head tiebreak).

The failure taxonomy maps onto the multi-agent failure analyses: MAST's verification and specification
categories \citep{Cemri-2025aa} cover glimmer's read-only paralysis and protocol confusion at the single-worker
level; the no-op-edit loops of night~2 and tonight's sixteen-fold retry are the ``cognitive deadlocks'' of
\citet{Liu-2025aa}; infinite-loop analyses find such loops in the agent--harness feedback path rather than the
model alone \citep{Hou-2026aa}, which is where the loop guard and the repair hook operate; and the attribution
dispute --- users propose guardrails, developers blame the model --- is exactly the split of
Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy} into harness-repairable and model-side classes \citep{Chen-2026aa}. What the published
work on quantisation and tool use does not contain is the setting itself: quantised candidates graded by builds
and hidden tests, paired arms on one GPU beside normal work, with the serving defects reported as first-class
results.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{A resident coder exists now, with two candidates for the seat.} Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B at 8~bit passes three
of the four task classes on both prompt styles, replicates the view-decimals plan pass, and was the first local
model through box-press on both arms; it holds the night's only build-verified passes. Its two caveats are
exactly what the head-to-head weighed: 35.5 of 36\,GB resident leaves no room for a second model server, ever,
on this machine --- the resident model is a commitment --- and 22.8\,s per turn is twice the operator's patience
for a one-line fix, which the 12--15\,min log-size passes make concrete.

\textbf{Half the memory passes the same tasks --- and now holds rank 1.} The second family's quant lift is the
practically important half of the quantisation finding: local30x8 matches ornith8's 5-of-6 cells at 17--18\,GB
resident and finishes its passes in 5--6.5 minutes against 12--25, and the log-size head-to-head then broke the
tie on both axes (4 of 4 gradeable against 3 of 4, at 3.8--4.4$\times$ the walls). The seat's one provisional
respect --- grading mode --- is settled: the daylight build pass verified all six deciding cells through the
builder, so the resident-coder deal of the night is the smaller model, and the 35B arm's crown was an artefact
of grading strength rather than of capability.

\textbf{Memory beyond the machine is a wall-clock problem.} The SSD experiments answer a question the resident
debate usually blurs: a 125B model that does not fit can still be the second-best coder of the night on quality
per task attempted --- with three load fixes, 80\,G of weights served productively from SSD, swap flat --- but
every decode token pays random expert-row page-ins, and at 3\,tok/s every 30-minute cap fills with reading. The
80B variant shows the same tax with the weights never resident at all. The conclusion is not ``bigger is
possible'' but ``bigger needs RAM'': the SSD-paged model is the likely new rank~1 on a host where it fits
(128\,G-class, or a smaller pack), and on this host it is a research instrument, not a worker.

\textbf{The cloud keeps the multi-file class.} save-model-ref (3 files, +64 lines) has now been attempted by
every local candidate and passed by none: 0 of 12 graded local arms on night~2, 0 of 2 tonight, with the rep2
attempt voided by the wedge --- 14 graded tries in total (Wilson 0--22\,\%). Sonnet~5.5 passes it in 7.0 minutes.
The division of labour stands: local resident for one- and two-function fixes on both prompt styles, cloud for
reference-preserving multi-file work.

\textbf{Instruction-following is the gate, not the weights.} Glimmer had the best streams of the night and the
worst table; the dense-30B architecture is thereby acquitted on speed, and the next candidate queue should be
ordered by agentic instruction-following evidence, not by tokens per second or density. The three glimmer failure
modes are also a warning about evaluation: a single-turn codegen score would have ranked it first.

\textbf{Replication rule earns its keep four times.} The view-decimals split replicated exactly (precise fail, plan
pass, both repetitions), which is what makes the plan-prompt pattern six-for-six locally --- until ornith6's
double view-decimals failure breaks it at 6~bit, a reminder that the pattern too is quant-dependent; the
wedge aborted the rest of rep~2 without touching the ranking, because the rule forbids single-run verdicts in
either direction; the morning's round 9 caught a pass on its way to becoming a claim --- ddalcu's
box-press precise win dissolved on re-run (37 cap-out turns, 0 files), a cap-edge variance the rule refuses
to promote; and the afternoon's completed grid re-ran the third family's every task on the unchanged recipe,
holding 5 of 6 to 10 of 12 over two reps (the box-press plan miss repeating, everything else passing again)
while flipping ornith6's night-2 precise fail the other way (Table~\ref{tab:logrep}).

\section{Recommendation}\label{sec:recommendation}

Load Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B 8-bit (local30x8) as the resident coder: one server, thinking off, output cap 8192,
prefix cache on, the qwen3 parsers and 8\,GB cache budget of the round-7 launcher, path and tool-name hooks
registered before the first spawn. Its evidence: 5 of 6 on the three-task set, log-size 4 of 4 across both
repetitions, and the head-to-head won on both axes at 3.8--4.4$\times$ ornith8's walls, at 17--18\,GB resident
in round 6 and 30.9--32.4\,GB in the round-7 window --- plan for the higher figure. Give it either prompt on
one-function fixes and the precise prompt first on view-decimals and box-press, with the plan-arm sprawl noted.
The seat's one condition is settled: the daylight build pass verified all six deciding cells (log-size 4 of 4,
box-press precise 2 of 2, both repetitions) with build and test exit 0 --- the deal is final, and the evidence
now matches the 35B's.

Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B 8-bit drops to the fallback seat and keeps a real edge: its rep-1 passes and the rep-2 plan
arm are build-verified (log-size 3 of 4), and it holds the night's only clean box-press sweep (2 of 2 against
local30x8's precise-only 2 of 2). If a rep flips a local30x8 cell, the resident role reverts to
ornith8 (one server, fold-text launcher, thinking off, output cap 8192, prefix cache on, 36\,GB watch line):
plan prompt on view-decimals-class tasks, either prompt on one-function fixes, box-press with the precise
prompt first.

Keep the save-model-ref class and any multi-file, reference-preserving change on the cloud coder (Sonnet~5.5);
no local model has crossed it in 14 graded tries, and the class now carries a second strike: it is also the
wedge trigger. Keep qwen38 as the light fallback (${\sim}$20\,GB) with its record now complete (3 of 8) and its
dense decode visibly slower than the MoE arms under two parallel agents; do not adopt glimmer or gpt-oss-20b on
this stack. Do not serve models larger than memory as workers until the prefix-cache and paging questions are
solved --- treat ddalcu's 3 of 6 as the upper bound of what SSD paging gives for free, and its box-press
non-replication as the caution that its rep-1 pass was cap-edge variance. For the next run: ornith8's rep-2
completion on the remaining three tasks is optional; add the Metal-peak watch line.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

The ranking stands on rep-1 evidence with the replicated cells marked (the head-to-head's log-size rep 2,
local30x8's box-press and view-decimals reps, glm47's log-size rep, ddalcu's non-replicated box-press rep 2,
and the afternoon rounds that gave qwen36x8 two reps on every task and ornith6 its log-size rep,
Table~\ref{tab:logrep}); 5 of 8 and
7 of 17 are small fractions of small denominators, and the Wilson intervals overlap for the middle ranks. Most
arms were graded buildless, and the daylight pass re-graded only rank~1's deciding cells (log-size 4 of 4,
box-press precise 2 of 2 --- the same diffs, nothing re-run): the seat is build-verified on those six cells,
but rank~1's round-6 view-decimals passes, qwen36x8's entire 10-of-12 record and every rank-3/4 pass remain
inspected diff-level checks
containing the true fixes, not green test suites --- and the runner-up keeps the richer build-verified record
(rep 1 plus the rep-2 plan arm; log-size 3 of 4). One of the diff-graded rows (codernext
log-size) self-reported failure because it could not build --- the fraction is a lower bound on capability and an
upper bound on verified quality. The two round-7 candidates were rejected before serving (model format; no
quant fits free RAM) and are therefore unmeasured, not failed. The 4-bit arm's 17 graded arms were collected under night-2's serving, so the
A/B compares bit widths across nights --- launcher and cache fixes are common to both, but not every serving
detail is identical; the night-1 4-bit local30 comparison crosses harness versions as well. The deadline
structure differed across the queue (12--30\,min caps), so per-task fractions are comparable within a candidate
and only roughly across candidates. Glimmer's voids reduce its graded record to five arms, all fails, which is
enough to reject it as the resident but not to characterise it; two ornith6 one-turn arms were not inspected and
their class assignment is provisional. The wedge was measured twice in effect but instrumented once; its trigger
conditions (long context, continuous batching, 35--45 minutes) are an observation, not an isolated cause. The
tool-error log for the night also contains 175 rows from an out-of-round infrastructure session; they are
excluded from every count here. Grading is mechanical, prompts and plans were written by cloud models, and a
cloud model is also the yardstick. All samples come from one subsystem of one code base on one machine.
